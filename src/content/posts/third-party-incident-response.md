Incident detection and response lets an organization quickly find and triage events to determine whether activity on its network is malicious. When companies work with third-party vendors or acquire other businesses, they need an agreement about how they'll cooperate on security. Some organizations respond to incidents far better than others, and that should factor into the decision to work together.

## Put it in writing

There should be a written agreement covering what access each company gets and what each must report.

- **Acquisitions:** the acquiring company may get visibility into the acquired company's network and devices.
- **Vendors and partners:** networks usually aren't integrated, so agreements should instead require reporting known incidents to each other, and may set requirements for intrusion detection coverage or alert response times.

Partners need these agreements to trust that the other side won't become an attack vector. There's also reputation: if one partner is compromised, the other can get pulled in whether or not its side was at fault.

## What should small businesses be expected to have?

I don't believe small businesses need mature, in-house detection and response. But if they want to work with large companies, they should have some form of incident response, internal or outsourced, that can find potential compromises. They should agree to fix any compromise and report it to the larger company.

In turn, the larger company should be able to support the smaller one when asked, as defined in their contract. Security changes quickly, and many small businesses can't afford a dedicated team, but they still need something in place.
