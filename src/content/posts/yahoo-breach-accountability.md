The Yahoo breach exposed "the usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, birthdates, passwords and security questions/answers for at least 500 million Yahoo accounts" ([National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/hacked-hacker-hire-lessons-yahoo-data-breaches-so-far)). Exposure on that scale is part of why the breach was hidden and so few users were notified.

## Were the penalties enough?

Yahoo paid a $35 million SEC penalty, the first time the SEC fined a public company for failing to disclose a breach, and $80 million to settle a federal securities class action (NLR). Those are large numbers, and I think they're fair. Yahoo faced a real dilemma: notify users and lose customers now, or protect that revenue and risk penalties later.

But I'm not sure fines will ever be effective until they're a meaningful share of a company's annual revenue. Otherwise they're a slap on the wrist. Accountability needs more than fines, and laws like the EU's GDPR and California's CCPA are slowly moving in that direction.

The FTC has also been holding companies accountable for breaches and privacy more broadly, both through fines and by requiring them to build and maintain security programs.

## Was it a cover-up?

I believe Yahoo intentionally tried to cover up the breach. The fact that it didn't broadly notify affected users, while informing only a tiny number of the hundreds of millions affected, shows it was trying to hide what had happened.

## The password-reset mistake

Yahoo's initial decision not to force password resets was a significant mistake. When investigating whether accounts were compromised, resetting passwords is almost always part of the process. Even when compromise is uncertain, a precautionary reset reduces the chance an attacker can get back in.

Here, Yahoo *knew* accounts were compromised and passwords stolen, and still didn't force resets. That's the epitome of a company not doing its due diligence, and it put users at further risk, especially those who reused the same credentials on other sites.
