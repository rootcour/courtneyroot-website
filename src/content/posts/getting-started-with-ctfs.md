Let's start with some background. Capture-the-flag events (CTFs) in the cybersecurity industry are usually put on by companies to find talent and to give hackers a place to practice their skills. These events often come with awards or prizes for completing the most tasks or finding the most "flags." In my opinion, CTFs are more like scavenger hunts than the actual game of capture the flag: players are given clues and must find the answers that lead to a hidden flag.

## Formats

There are a few styles of CTF:

- **Jeopardy.** The board looks like the TV show, and teams solve questions to earn points.
- **Question and answer.** Questions are grouped into sections by topic. For example, a reconnaissance section might ask about an organization using information found on the internet or the company website.
- **Immersive gameplay.** A rarer style where the organizers build a small world you travel around, interacting with characters to solve each step.

Almost all CTFs assign points to steps or answers. When you complete a task, you earn the points and move up the rankings.

## Why it's worth it

Starting out can be daunting, especially if you don't know anyone or what to expect. But CTFs are a great way to find out where your skills are and grow them. The more you play, the more you learn and the more tools you pick up. Anyone can sign up, so joining is the first step. Many events are designed for college students and entry-level professionals, and some are aimed at high school students.

## Common challenge types

- **Reconnaissance:** finding information about a person or company that could reveal a weakness.
- **Network scanning:** using command-line tools like Nmap with the right flags, or digging through Wireshark packet captures.
- **Steganography:** finding hidden messages inside sound, image or video files.
- **Encoding and encryption:** converting messages between encodings (like Base64) or decrypting them.

This may sound overwhelming, but most of these can be solved with free, publicly available tools:

- [Wireshark](https://www.wireshark.org/) for reading network traffic, with plenty of tutorials online.
- [CyberChef](https://gchq.github.io/CyberChef/) for decoding, decrypting and converting data.

## Where to find them

Cybersecurity companies sponsor or run CTFs to find people with the skills they want to hire. Many security conferences also run CTFs for attendees, which is a great way to play while also attending talks.

Many events open with an introduction video or talk to help newcomers get set up. Depending on the organizer, they may walk through a few questions to show how they approach a puzzle. That's especially helpful if you've never played before.

## One more tip

Add the events you play to your resume. They show that you invest time outside of work and classes in continued learning and in the security community.
