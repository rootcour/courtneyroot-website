# LinkedIn: Attackers used to steal your data. Now they want your tokens.

**Post on:** Dec 15, 2026
**Blog post:** https://courtneyroot.dev/writing/attackers-want-your-tokens/

## Post

After a breach, the first question used to be "what data did they take?" In 2026, attackers are going after something else: tokens.

Two ways I've seen them cash in:

1. Stolen AI API keys as free compute. Attackers resell access to someone else's AI account, or use it to power their own hacking tools. The owner of the key pays the bill.
2. Stolen OAuth tokens as standing access. AI tools hold tokens for your Gmail, GitHub and Slack. When attackers broke into Context.ai and Composio this year, they went after the tokens those companies held for their customers.

A stolen file is a snapshot. A stolen token keeps working, often survives a password reset, and lets the attacker act as you.

What I'd do:
• Set spending limits and alerts on every AI provider key
• Audit the apps connected to your Google or Microsoft accounts
• Ask AI vendors how they store your tokens
• Practice revoking access in minutes
