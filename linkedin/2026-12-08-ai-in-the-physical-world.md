## Post

Most AI security problems are about data. When an agent controls a drone, a lab robot or a building system, the damage can be physical, and you can't roll it back.

Earlier in my career I worked on product security for aviation products. Four habits from that world that I think apply directly to AI agents:

1. Design for the failure, not just the attack. Know what the system does when something goes wrong.
2. Keep safety limits separate from the AI making decisions, so they hold even when it's wrong.
3. Earn autonomy with evidence. Start with a person approving each step and loosen it as the record builds.
4. Record everything, and know which agent did it.
